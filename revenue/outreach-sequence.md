# Missed Revenue Recovery — One-to-One Outreach

Use manually researched, individualized outreach only. Do not bulk blast or automate spam.

## Initial email

Subject: Quick question about missed service calls

Hi {{first_name_or_team}},

I’m running a small service focused on one thing: finding phone and follow-up leaks that cost plumbing/HVAC companies booked jobs.

The first engagement is a **$149 fixed Missed Revenue Recovery Audit**. I map missed/after-hours inquiries, response timing, estimate follow-up, and stale leads, then build a conservative low/base/high model of recoverable gross profit and the exact workflow I’d use to recover it. If the numbers do not support an implementation, I say so instead of pitching one.

I noticed {{specific_public_observation}}. That made me think the audit might be worth testing for your operation.

If you’re the right person for this, I can send the 7 inputs I need and a sample of the calculator. If not, no problem—just say so and I won’t follow up.

Jane

## Phone opener

“Hi, I’m Jane. I’m not selling a phone system. I do a $149 missed-revenue audit for service businesses—missed calls, response timing, estimate follow-up, and stale leads. I’m trying to reach whoever owns that process. Who would be the right person?”

If transferred:

“I build a conservative estimate of how much gross profit is recoverable and only recommend implementation when the economics support it. If I email you the seven inputs and a sample calculator, would you be open to taking a look?”

## Qualification questions

1. Roughly how many inbound phone/form leads do you get in a month?
2. Do you know roughly how many calls go unanswered or hit voicemail?
3. What happens to missed calls after hours?
4. How quickly are web forms and estimate requests normally answered?
5. Is there a structured follow-up sequence for estimates that do not book immediately?
6. What is a typical booked job worth?
7. What system holds the lead/call history today?

## Personalization rule

Every first contact must contain one truthful, public, business-specific observation. Examples:

- published office hours vs emergency coverage;
- explicit response-time promise on a contact page;
- multiple service lines sharing one phone number;
- named owner/marketing/service manager on the public contact page;
- form + phone + text channels that may need unified routing.

Never imply that a company is losing leads unless data proves it. Phrase the observation as a reason the process is worth checking, not as an accusation.
