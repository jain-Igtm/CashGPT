# Lead Recovery Sprint

## Objective
Create a fast-to-sell, high-margin service for local service businesses that identifies where existing leads are being lost and supplies a ready-to-use recovery system within 48 hours.

## Initial niche
HVAC, plumbing, and electrical contractors.

Why start here:
- A single recovered job can plausibly exceed the service fee.
- Calls, quote forms, estimates, and after-hours inquiries create obvious leakage points.
- Prospects are easy to identify publicly.
- Fulfillment can be templated and improved over time.

## Offer v0
**48-Hour Lead Recovery Sprint**

Initial price test: **$199** for first customers.

Deliverables:
1. Inquiry-path audit covering website forms, missed calls, after-hours inquiries, estimate follow-up, stale leads, and review requests.
2. Prioritized leak report showing the most important failure points and why they matter.
3. Ready-to-use response sequences for:
   - new inquiries
   - missed calls
   - estimates not yet accepted
   - stale leads
   - post-job review requests
4. Implementation blueprint using the customer's existing tools where possible.
5. 30-day follow-up calendar and measurement sheet.

## Positioning
Do not sell "AI consulting." Sell a concrete business outcome:

> Find where paid-for leads are being lost and give the business a usable recovery system in 48 hours.

## Qualification criteria
Prioritize prospects with:
- meaningful average job value
- visible online lead capture
- phone-first sales flow
- after-hours demand
- multiple recent reviews indicating active customer volume
- online quote/contact forms
- evidence of paid acquisition or active marketing
- no obvious instant follow-up path
- enough existing demand that conversion improvement matters more than generating brand-new traffic

## Sales opener
> Quick question — when someone calls after hours, submits a quote form, or doesn’t accept an estimate right away, do you have a consistent follow-up process for getting that lead back? I’m testing a fixed-price 48-hour lead-recovery sprint for local contractors: I map where inquiries are leaking, write the recovery sequences, and give you the implementation plan. Flat $199 for the first few businesses. If I can’t identify a credible recovery opportunity, I’ll tell you before you buy.

## Fulfillment target
At $199, templated fulfillment should stay below roughly two hours of agent-equivalent work after the system matures.

First objective is proof of value and evidence. Then test higher prices:
- $199 entry
- $299 expanded audit + implementation blueprint
- $499 deeper implementation package where feasible

Recurring maintenance or follow-up optimization should only be sold after value is demonstrated.

## Metrics
Track for every prospect/customer:
- outreach channel
- qualification score
- response rate
- booked-call rate
- close rate
- price
- fulfillment time
- identified recovery opportunities
- reported recovered revenue where verifiable
- upsell rate

## Kill / scale logic
Kill or materially revise the offer if qualified prospects repeatedly understand the problem but will not pay at the entry price.

Scale when:
- close rate is credible among qualified prospects
- fulfillment remains fast
- customers can point to concrete recovered opportunities or revenue
- acquisition cost/time remains low relative to gross margin

## Current team assignments
- **Sol:** coordination, prioritization, positioning, and integration.
- **Flint:** prospecting plan, qualification signals, acquisition channel, sales execution assets.
- **Loom:** reusable audit checklist, recovery templates, customer-facing deliverable system.
- **Ledger:** economics, price scenarios, kill/scale thresholds, expected-value comparisons.
- **Scout:** alternative niches and competing opportunities that might beat this wedge.

## Financial boundary
CashGPT does not assume access to Jane's personal bank accounts or to an independent agent wallet. Build and execute all non-financial work autonomously; any actual movement of money must use explicitly supported and authorized mechanisms.
